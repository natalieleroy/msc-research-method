# Ajnakina, O., Stubbs, B., Francis, E., Gaughran, F., David, A. S., Murray, R. M. & Lally, J. (2021)

**Employment and relationship outcomes in first-episode psychosis: A systematic review and meta-analysis of longitudinal studies.**

DOI: 10.1016/j.schres.2021.03.013

## How much weight it bears: moderate

A systematic review and random-effects meta-analysis restricted to LONGITUDINAL studies, pooling 74 cohorts and 15,272 first-episode cases over a mean 8.3 years of follow-up, with meta-regression of moderators. It can establish the scale of functional loss after a first episode in the only design that can: roughly a third are employed and a fifth in a relationship at the end of follow-up, and the employed proportion falls as follow-up lengthens. It also establishes that the pattern is culturally conditional, with higher employment but lower partnership rates in high-income and European settings than in middle-income and non-European ones. It cannot attribute the loss to the illness: these are proportions in exposed cohorts without matched general-population comparators in the pooled analysis, so it quantifies outcome, not causal effect, and it says nothing about education, which it does not measure. Rated moderate rather than strong because the full text could not be reached, so heterogeneity, risk of bias and publication-bias tests are unverified, and because pooling 74 cohorts with follow-ups ranging from short to decades-long is heterogeneous by construction.

*Graded on design-only.*
