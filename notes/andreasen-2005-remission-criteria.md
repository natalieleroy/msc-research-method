# Andreasen, N. C., Carpenter, W. T., Kane, J. M., Lasser, R. A., Marder, S. R. & Weinberger, D. R. (2005)

**Remission in schizophrenia: proposed criteria and rationale for consensus**

DOI: 10.1176/appi.ajp.162.3.441

## How much weight it bears: indicative

The paper that established the first consensus-based operational criteria for symptomatic remission in schizophrenia, and the one almost every later cohort cites when it says a participant was 'in remission' - including `notes/okeeffe-2021-meaning-in-life-fep.md`, which applies these criteria at 20-year follow-up. It can establish what the field agreed remission means and why it chose thresholds maintained over time rather than change scores. It cannot establish that the criteria are valid, that they predict anything, or that they capture recovery as opposed to symptom level: the authors themselves call the approach novel and say it 'will require further examination of its validity and utility, as well as future refinement, particularly in relation to psychosocial and cognitive function and dysfunction'. Indicative, which is the right tier for an expert consensus statement used for a concept: it is authoritative about a definition and it is not evidence. Rated from the abstract alone - the full text is paywalled - so the specific item thresholds and the six-month duration requirement are recorded here only where a second source in the corpus states them.
