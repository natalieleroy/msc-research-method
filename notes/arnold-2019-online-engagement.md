# Arnold, C., Villagonzalo, K.-A., Meyer, D., Farhall, J., Foley, F., Kyrios, M. & Thomas, N. (2019)

**Predicting engagement with an online psychosocial intervention for psychosis: Exploring individual- and intervention-level predictors**

DOI: 10.1016/j.invent.2019.100266

## How much weight it bears: moderate

A prospective study of 98 adults with SCID-confirmed psychosis in which the key predictor, asynchronous email support, was randomised and engagement was measured objectively from website logs rather than self-report, so it can establish that light-touch human support raises use of a self-guided online intervention in this population and that externally controlled motivation moderates that effect. It cannot establish that more use produces better clinical outcomes, because no outcome was measured here and the authors say the dose-response relationship may not be linear; nor can it generalise to routine care, because the sample was research-recruited, internet-confident and screened. Moderate rather than strong: the randomised comparison is sound but the sample is small, the predictor set was analysed observationally, and one table is internally inconsistent (see Limitations).
