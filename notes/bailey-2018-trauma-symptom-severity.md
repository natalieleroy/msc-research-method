# Bailey, T., Alvarez-Jimenez, M., Garcia-Sanchez, A. M., Hulbert, C., Barlow, E. & Bendall, S. (2018)

**Childhood trauma is associated with severity of hallucinations and delusions in psychotic disorders: a systematic review and meta-analysis.**

DOI: 10.1093/schbul/sbx161

## How much weight it bears: moderate

A systematic review screening 6667 studies to 41, meta-analysing 29 with 4680 participants, with reporting bias and quality assessed and unpublished data sought from authors. It moves the trauma argument one step past risk of onset — which Varese et al. (2012) already covers — to severity of specific symptoms: childhood trauma correlates with hallucination severity (r = .199) and delusion severity (r = .172), both P < .001. It also delivers a result the authors did NOT expect and report as such: trauma was not correlated with negative-symptom severity (r = .049, P = .095), while neglect specifically was (r = .142, P = .005). It CANNOT establish that trauma shapes the CONTENT of psychotic experience — severity is not content, and nothing here addresses what the voices or delusions are about. Nor can it establish direction: the pooled studies are correlational, and the effects are small (r around .17 to .20, roughly 3-4% of variance). Moderate on design, with a clear ceiling on what it may be asked to say.

*Graded on design-only.*
