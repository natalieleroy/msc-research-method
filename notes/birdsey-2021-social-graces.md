# Birdsey, N. & Kustner, C. (2021)

**Reviewing the Social GRACES: What do they add and limit in systemic thinking and practice?**

DOI: 10.1080/01926187.2020.1830731

## How much weight it bears: indicative

A peer-reviewed review that traces the Social GRACES to named origins — Burnham (1992, 1993) and Roper-Hall (1998), via the earlier 'DISGRRACCE' mnemonic — lists the current fifteen graces, and then sets out four substantive criticisms: that the framework is restrictively linear, that it is reductive and risks becoming 'a list of areas of difference', that therapists privilege the graces inside their own comfort zone, and that the current fifteen omit disablism. It can establish what the GRACES are, where they came from and what they are criticised for, which is exactly what a cultural-considerations paragraph needs. It CANNOT establish that using them changes clinical outcomes: there is no trial, no outcome measure and no psychosis population anywhere in it, and the single clinical illustration is one anonymised supervision case written up by the authors. Indicative on design.

*Graded on full-appraisal.*
