# Chadwick, P., Williams, C. & Mackenzie, J. (2003)

**Impact of case formulation in cognitive behaviour therapy for psychosis.**

DOI: 10.1016/s0005-7967(02)00033-5

## How much weight it bears: indicative

The only empirical test of case formulation in psychosis I could locate, and it is a largely NULL and partly adverse result: across two experiments, formulation 'had little or no impact' on perceived therapeutic relationship, on strength of delusional and self-evaluative beliefs, or on anxiety and depression; therapists' own ratings of the relationship improved while clients' did not, and six clients found formulation at least in part a negative experience. It can therefore establish that a formulation-led plan should not be presented as an intervention with demonstrated benefit, which is a real constraint on `e2-plan`. It CANNOT establish that formulation is useless — it is small, uncontrolled, single-site, from one service, over twenty years old and predates the Five Ps version of the framework, and the abstract does not report the sample size. Indicative on design; its FIT to the claim that a formulation-based plan is justified is high precisely because it qualifies it.

*Graded on design-only.*
