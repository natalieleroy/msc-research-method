# Cohen, A., Naslund, J. A., Chang, S., Nagendra, S., Bhan, A., Rozatkar, A., Thirthalli, J., Bondre, A., Tugnawat, D., Reddy, P. V., Dutt, S., Choudhary, S., Chand, P. K., Patel, V., Keshavan, M., Joshi, D., Mehta, U. M. & Torous, J. (2023)

**Relapse prediction in schizophrenia with smartphone digital phenotyping during COVID-19: a prospective, three-site, two-country, longitudinal study.**

DOI: 10.1038/s41537-023-00332-5

## How much weight it bears: moderate

A prospective, three-site, two-country smartphone cohort (132 participants, 76 with schizophrenia and 56 controls, mean 156 days of enrolment) in which 20 clinical relapses occurred and were ascertained from interviews and medical records. It can establish that passive smartphone data behave differently around relapse in a real outpatient population in both the United States and India - anomalies 2.12 times more frequent in the month before relapse and 2.78 times more frequent in the month around it, and 1.41 times more effective than a survey-only logistic regression - and it can establish, with the authors' own permutation test, that the method did not differ significantly between the Boston, Bangalore and Bhopal sites. It cannot establish that this is usable as a clinical alert: of 188 statistically significant anomalies, only 13 (6.9%) were true positives, so roughly 93% of flags were false, and the model was fitted retrospectively to prospectively collected data rather than deployed and tested. Rated moderate: the design is as close to a service test as this literature gets, 20 relapse events is a small outcome count, and the COVID-19 data collection window is a limitation the authors state at length.
