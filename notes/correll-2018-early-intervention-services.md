# Correll, C. U., Galling, B., Pawar, A., Krivko, A., Bonetto, C., Ruggeri, M., Craig, T. J., Nordentoft, M. et al. (2018)

**Comparison of early intervention services vs treatment as usual for early-phase psychosis: a systematic review, meta-analysis, and meta-regression.**

DOI: 10.1001/jamapsychiatry.2018.0623

## How much weight it bears: strong

A PRISMA-reported systematic review and meta-analysis of 10 RCTs and 2176 patients, with prespecified subgroup and meta-regression analyses and unpublished data obtained from the authors of every included trial, reporting risk ratios and standardised mean differences with 95% confidence intervals. It is the best available evidence that a coordinated multimodal service — not any single modality — outperforms ordinary care in early-phase psychosis, and its inclusion criteria define that service as combining psychosocial AND psychopharmacological components delivered by one team, which is the direct evidence that a plan touching several domains beats a referral chain. It CANNOT establish which component does the work: the unit of randomisation is the service, so the meta-analysis is silent on the contribution of medication, family work, vocational support or psychotherapy taken separately. It also cannot speak beyond 24 months, the longest trial duration included, and the protocol was 'predetermined but unpublished' rather than registered, which is a transparency weakness in an otherwise well-conducted review.

*Graded on full-appraisal.*
