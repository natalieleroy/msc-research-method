# Dagnino, F. M., Fante, C., Guerrieri, V. & Passarelli, M. (2026)

**Addicted, attached, or just delegating? A scoping review on 'problematic artificial intelligence use'**

DOI: 10.3389/fpsyg.2026.1900953

## How much weight it bears: moderate

A PRISMA-ScR scoping review with a registered protocol that maps the whole construct of problematic AI use across 37 empirical studies, and the only source found that answers the measurement question directly. It CAN establish that the construct exists in the literature but is not settled: terminology is inconsistent, definitions are frequently absent altogether in eight of the included studies, AI-specific instruments do exist — the Problematic ChatGPT Use Scale, built on the DSM-5-TR Internet Gaming Disorder framework, and the AI Chatbot Dependence Scale — but most measurement borrows scales for social media, smartphones, mobile applications, computers and in one case fast food, which the authors argue 'presuppose rather than test' that problematic AI use has the same structure. It can also establish that the evidence base is 30 quantitative and 7 mixed-method studies, overwhelmingly cross-sectional. It CANNOT establish prevalence, direction or any causal claim, and the authors say so: 'The predominance of cross-sectional studies limits conclusions about the directionality of key associations ... precludes conclusions about whether, for instance, loneliness leads to problematic AI use or whether problematic AI use exacerbates loneliness.' Moderate rather than strong: a transparent, registered scoping review with a careful construct critique, but a scoping review performs no risk-of-bias assessment by design, searched only Web of Science and Scopus, and was restricted to English.
