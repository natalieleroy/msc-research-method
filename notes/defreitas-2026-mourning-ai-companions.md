# De Freitas, J., Castelo, N., Uğuralp, A. K. & Oğuz-Uğuralp, Z. (2026)

**Mourning the loss of AI companions**

DOI: 10.1038/s41562-026-02569-3

## How much weight it bears: moderate

Two natural experiments on real platform changes — Replika withdrawing erotic role play and OpenAI replacing the model behind ChatGPT — which is a design nobody can run deliberately and which the AI-and-mental-health literature otherwise lacks entirely. It CAN establish that withdrawing or altering a companion system is followed by measurable increases in negativity, loss framing and restoration desire in what users write (Replika +24.7 percentage points, 95% CI 20.1 to 29.2; ChatGPT +13.0, CI 10.8 to 15.2), with large differences in sadness (d = 2.67 vs 1.41) and self-reported negative mental health (d = 1.72 vs 0.63), and that Replika users rated closeness above common human ties (versus friend d = 0.47). It CANNOT establish how common any of this is, because the samples are self-selected forum members and survey respondents recruited from a population already attached enough to post about it, so every figure is conditional on being in that group; nor can it separate grief from the ordinary annoyance of a product change, since the comparison is between two platform changes rather than against a non-bereaved control. There is no clinical measure and no psychosis outcome. Moderate: a genuinely quasi-experimental design with confidence intervals and a very large text corpus in a strong venue, against severe selection, outcomes derived from public posts rather than instruments, and a record read from the abstract only.

*Graded on design-only.*
