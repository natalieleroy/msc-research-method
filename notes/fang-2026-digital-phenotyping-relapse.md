# Fang, S.-S. & Chen, S.-H. (2026)

**Digital phenotyping for predicting relapse in psychiatric disorders: a systematic review of passive sensing approaches.**

DOI: 10.1186/s12888-026-08157-z

## How much weight it bears: indicative

A PRISMA 2020 and SWiM systematic review of 52 passive-sensing relapse-prediction studies (4,814 participants) that applies PROBAST and reaches a usefully sceptical conclusion: 75% of studies at high risk of bias, only 10 of 52 externally validated, external validation lowering AUC by about 0.08-0.10, and not one of the 52 studies showing that deploying a prediction model improved any patient outcome. Those negative findings are the reason to hold it. It is rated INDICATIVE rather than moderate because its own reporting cannot be trusted at the level of its citations: the abstract cites 'the modified Newcastle-Ottawa Scale [16]' to a deep-learning paper and 'PROBAST [25, 26]' to two bipolar smartphone studies, while the body cites PROBAST as [22, 23], which are a voice-analysis paper and a circadian-phenotyping paper. Neither appraisal tool is correctly cited anywhere in the article. Add two authors from geriatrics and neurology rather than psychiatry, a three-database search excluding Embase, Scopus and Web of Science, no pooling, and a study-count table whose rows sum to 56 against 52 included studies, and the right use is as a signpost to its primary studies and as a statement of the field's validation deficit, not as an authority to be cited for a number.

*Graded on full-appraisal.*
