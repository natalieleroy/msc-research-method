# Fond, G., Vidal, M., Joseph, M., Etchecopar-Etchart, D., Solmi, M., Yon, D. K., Correll, C. U. & Boyer, L. (2023)

**Self-stigma in schizophrenia: a systematic review and meta-analysis of 37 studies from 25 high- and low-to-middle income countries.**

DOI: 10.1038/s41380-023-02003-4

## How much weight it bears: moderate

A PROSPERO-registered (CRD42020185030) random-effects meta-analysis with no language or time restriction, pooling 37 observational studies (n=7,717) from 25 countries that used one of two validated self-stigma scales, with subgroup and meta-regression analysis of associated factors. It can establish that self-stigma is measurable and substantial across six dimensions in schizophrenia-spectrum disorders, that pooled levels did not reduce over the 2007-2020 publication window, and that the correlates are social and iatrogenic (unemployment, low income, singleness, living outside urban areas, high antipsychotic dose, low functioning) rather than illness-severity indices, since psychotic severity, age at onset, illness duration, age, sex and education were NOT associated. It cannot establish direction: every included study is observational and the review reports association, so it cannot say whether stigma follows impaired functioning or helps produce it. Rated moderate rather than strong because the full text could not be reached, so no risk-of-bias assessment, heterogeneity statistic or funnel-plot analysis has been verified, and the pooled means are scale scores on a 1-4 range rather than an interpretable clinical threshold.

*Graded on design-only.*
