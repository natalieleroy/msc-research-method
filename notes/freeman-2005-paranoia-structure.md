# Freeman, D., Garety, P. A., Bebbington, P. E., Smith, B., Rollinson, R., Fowler, D., Kuipers, E., Ray, K. & Dunn, G. (2005)

**Psychological investigation of the structure of paranoia in a non-clinical population**

DOI: 10.1192/bjp.186.5.427

## How much weight it bears: moderate

A large (n=1202) multidimensional questionnaire survey that established the continuum position for paranoia: the count of suspicious thoughts followed a single continuous, near-exponential distribution with no distinct subpopulation, and rarer, odder suspicions sat on top of commoner emotional concerns in a hierarchy. It can establish that paranoid thinking is common and continuously distributed in a non-clinical population and that it is structured rather than random; it cannot establish prevalence in any general population, nor causal direction, because the sample is a self-selected student convenience sample recruited by e-mail and the design is cross-sectional — both of which the authors state. Moderate rather than strong because of the sampling, not the analysis.
