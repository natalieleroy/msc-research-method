# Gupta, T., Deam, M., Headley, E., Seah, T. H. S., Silk, J., Griffith, J. M., Vargas, T., Messineo, S. & Horton, L. (2026)

**Trajectories of distressing psychotic-like experience in youth: the interplay of recent negative life events and screen time**

DOI: 10.1017/S0954579426101588

## How much weight it bears: moderate

The only source found that puts adversity and digital media in the same prospective model with a psychosis-relevant outcome, which is exactly where the corpus's trauma strand and its technology strand fail to meet. Its strength is not uniform and should not be cited as if it were. The TRAJECTORY findings are moderate and rest on the full cohort: latent growth mixture modelling over five timepoints in 11867 ABCD participants gave an increasing PLE-distress class (n=409), a decreasing class (n=698) and a no-distress class (n=10759), and youth in the increasing class reported more recent negative life events while reporting similar total daily screen time to the decreasing class — so adversity, not screen volume, is what separates the two symptomatic trajectories. The MODERATION findings, which are the ones an essay would actually want, are indicative at best: the weekend screen time x negative life events interaction (B 0.51, SE 0.19, t 2.74, P=.007, 95% CI 0.17 to 0.86) comes from a mixed model with n=103, the weekday equivalent was NOT significant (P=.12), and the texting and social-media results the abstract leads with are labelled exploratory in the body and carry t(90). It CANNOT establish that screen use causes PLE distress, cannot speak to AI or chatbots at all, and its 4-year outcome data were released for only around 40% of the cohort with the increasing-distress trajectory differentially under-represented. Moderate overall, with the carve-out stated.
