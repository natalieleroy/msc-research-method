# Hardy, A., Emsley, R., Freeman, D., Bebbington, P., Garety, P. A., Kuipers, E. E., Dunn, G. & Fowler, D. (2016)

**Psychological mechanisms mediating effects between trauma and psychotic symptoms: the role of affect regulation, intrusive trauma memory, beliefs, and depression**

DOI: 10.1093/schbul/sbv175

## How much weight it bears: moderate

The best source found for the proposition that childhood adversity is associated with the TYPE of psychotic symptom and not only with risk or severity, and it is a clinical sample of 228 people with relapsing psychosis rather than a student or community survey. It CAN establish specificity in this sample: childhood emotional abuse was associated with persecutory and referential delusions while childhood sexual abuse was associated with auditory hallucinations, and the persecutory association was statistically mediated by negative beliefs about other people. It CANNOT establish temporal order or cause, and the authors say so themselves — 'The study was cross-sectional and future work should investigate these processes in prospective or longitudinal studies'; trauma was recalled retrospectively by people who already had psychosis, PTSD symptoms were self-reported rather than interviewed, and the mediation models for hallucinations rest on n = 118 complete cases, not 228. Moderate rather than strong: a well-specified a priori hypothesis set with a counterfactual mediation framework and a representativeness check against trial non-completers, against a cross-sectional design, retrospective recall, and a symptom coding that collapses 0-5 severity scales to present/absent.
