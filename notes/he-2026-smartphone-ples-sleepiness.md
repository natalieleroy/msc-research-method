# He, M., Ding, X., Liu, X. & Wang, D. (2026)

**Longitudinal bidirectional relationships between problematic smartphone use and psychotic-like experiences among young adults: the mediating role of excessive daytime sleepiness**

DOI: 10.1016/j.psychres.2026.117401

## How much weight it bears: moderate

The only prospective study found anywhere on a technology the corpus's argument is actually about: smartphone use and psychotic-like experiences, three waves at six-month intervals, 6153 young adults, with within-person modelling that separates change in a person from differences between people. It CAN establish temporal order in this sample, and the order runs the way the argument does not want: after accounting for stable between-person differences the random-intercept model 'provided no evidence for a bidirectional association', and the surviving within-person path was increases in psychotic-like experiences at Time 1 predicting increases in problematic smartphone use at Time 3 through daytime sleepiness at Time 2. It CANNOT establish anything about generative AI, chatbots or conversational agents — the exposure is problematic smartphone use as a global construct — nor about diagnosed psychosis, since the outcome is self-reported psychotic-like experiences in a non-clinical student sample. Moderate rather than strong: a large, genuinely prospective, within-person design with the right comparison of CLPM against RI-CLPM, against entirely self-reported exposure and outcome, an 79.7% female sample, a single-country single-population frame, and a record read from the abstract only, so no effect sizes, intervals or attrition have been checked.

*Graded on design-only.*
