# Howes, O. D. & Kapur, S. (2009)

**The dopamine hypothesis of schizophrenia: version III--the final common pathway**

DOI: 10.1093/schbul/sbp006

## How much weight it bears: moderate

The field's canonical statement of the current dopamine hypothesis, peer reviewed in Schizophrenia Bulletin, open access, and the paper that explicitly ties dopamine dysfunction to psychotic experience through aberrant salience - the mechanism `notes/bokhari-2026-technology-delusions.md` already leans on without the corpus holding a primary for it. It can establish what the hypothesis now claims, that elevated presynaptic striatal dopamine synthesis capacity is the most widely replicated dopaminergic abnormality in schizophrenia with reported effect sizes of 0.63 to 1.89 across seven of nine studies, that striatal dopamine elevation is absent in mania, depression and non-psychotic psychiatric disorders, and that the authors locate the abnormality with **psychosis** rather than with schizophrenia as a diagnosis. It cannot establish causation, direction or sequence: it is a selective review of cross-sectional case-control PET studies, the measures are indirect indices rather than dopamine levels, and the authors say the hypothesis 'will be revised as more data become available'. Moderate rather than strong because it is a selective narrative synthesis with no stated search strategy or risk-of-bias assessment, and because the imaging evidence it rests on is almost entirely small-sample - see the appraisal against Marek et al. below.
