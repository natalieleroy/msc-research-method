# Jauhar, S., McKenna, P. J., Radua, J., Fung, E., Salvador, R. & Laws, K. R. (2014)

**Cognitive-behavioural therapy for the symptoms of schizophrenia: systematic review and meta-analysis with examination of potential bias**

DOI: 10.1192/bjp.bp.112.116285

## How much weight it bears: strong

A PRISMA systematic review with random-effects meta-analysis of 52 randomised trials, pre-specified moderator analyses for three Cochrane risk-of-bias domains plus use of a control intervention, and three separate publication-bias tests: it is the primary source for every effect size the 2019 editorial reports, and it is the strongest treatment-efficacy source now in the corpus. It establishes that pooled end-of-treatment effects of CBT on overall, positive and negative symptoms in schizophrenia lie in the small range and that masking of outcome assessment significantly moderates them, with positive symptoms falling to non-significance in masked trials. Its own stated limits are real: the low-risk-of-bias subanalyses rest on 8-11 studies because most trials were rated 'unclear' rather than low or high risk, and it did not examine effects on depression, anxiety or distress, so it can say nothing about those. Full appraisal from the full text; strong on design and directly on the claim.
