# Kirkbride, J. B., Jones, P. B., Ullrich, S. & Coid, J. W. (2014)

**Social deprivation, inequality, and the neighborhood-level incidence of psychotic syndromes in East London.**

DOI: 10.1093/schbul/sbs151

## How much weight it bears: moderate

A population-based first-episode incidence study using Bayesian hierarchical models with spatially structured neighbourhood random effects, which is the appropriate design for this question, and it separates three correlated exposures that are usually confounded: income inequality (RR 1.25, 95% CI 1.04-1.49 per SD), absolute deprivation (RR 1.28, 95% CI 1.08-1.51) and population density (RR 1.18, 95% CI 1.00-1.41), each independently associated with non-affective psychosis after standardising for individual age, sex, ethnicity and socioeconomic status. It is the only source in the corpus in which deprivation predicts PSYCHOSIS rather than general psychopathology, which is precisely the hole in `e1-predispose`. It CANNOT establish causation, and cannot separate social causation from social drift — people with emerging psychosis may move into deprived areas. The density interval reaches 1.00, no neighbourhood-level variation was found for the AFFECTIVE psychoses, and it is a single city over two years with 427 cases, so the neighbourhood-level estimates rest on small numbers per area.

*Graded on design-only.*
