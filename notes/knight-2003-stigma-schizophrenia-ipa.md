# Knight, M. T. D., Wykes, T. & Hayward, P. (2003)

**'People don't understand': An investigation of stigma in schizophrenia using Interpretative Phenomenological Analysis (IPA)**

DOI: 10.1080/0963823031000118203

## How much weight it bears: indicative

An early interpretative phenomenological analysis whose stated purpose was 'to provide an account of the life experiences of persons with schizophrenia', which is the kind of evidence this corpus is thinnest on, and which generated super-ordinate themes of judgement, comparison and personal understanding, with stigma appearing as both public stigma and self-stigma. It can indicate how a small number of people with a schizophrenia diagnosis described being judged and judging themselves, and it is the earliest qualitative account of psychosis the corpus holds. It cannot establish how common any of that is, and it cannot speak to onset, first episode or risk at all - stigma is the phenomenon, not psychosis. Indicative rather than moderate on three counts: six participants; no full text read, so the sampling, analytic rigour and reflexivity are entirely unverified; and the abstract reports no recruitment setting, no country, no age range and no illness duration, so there is no way to judge who these six people were.

*Graded on design-only.*
