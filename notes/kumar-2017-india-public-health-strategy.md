# Kumar, C. N., Thirthalli, J., Suresha, K. K., Venkatesh, B. K., Arunachala, U. & Gangadhar, B. N. (2017)

**Antipsychotic treatment, psychoeducation & regular follow up as a public health strategy for schizophrenia: results from a prospective study.**

DOI: 10.4103/ijmr.IJMR_838_15

## How much weight it bears: indicative

A prospective community cohort in rural south India reporting that 70.6% (95% CI 64.35-76.95) achieved clinical remission and 69.6% (95% CI 63.29-76.07) a satisfactory outcome after about four years of ensured continuous antipsychotics plus low-intensity psychoeducation, with disability falling from 66.7% to 27.3% of the cohort. It is valuable as one of very few sources combining pharmacological and educational components in a non-Western population, and the authors' framing — that a minority need structured psychosocial work beyond this — is a usable qualification. It CANNOT support an efficacy claim: there is no control group, no randomisation and no comparison period, so remission cannot be attributed to the intervention rather than to natural course, regression to the mean or ascertainment; the two components are bundled and cannot be separated; n = 201 in one taluk; and the published abstract is garbled at the disability p-value ('PInterpretation'), so that statistic is NOT recorded here. Indicative because the design cannot carry more.

*Graded on design-only.*
