# Kvig, E. I. & Nilssen, S. (2023)

**Does method matter? Assessing the validity and clinical utility of structured diagnostic interviews among a clinical sample of first-admitted patients with psychosis: A replication study.**

DOI: 10.3389/fpsyt.2023.1076299

## How much weight it bears: moderate

A naturalistic replication in a consecutive, catchment-based first-admission psychosis sample, comparing SCID-I/P diagnoses made by a trained non-clinician against best-estimate consensus diagnoses assigned by a multidisciplinary team using all available information, with kappa, sensitivity and specificity and a cross-tabulation reported in full. It can establish that in this real clinical setting a fully structured interview agreed poorly with the consensus standard (kappa 0.21), missed 24 of 44 schizophrenia-spectrum cases, and generated two or more comorbid diagnoses in 49% of patients, and it can establish that this replicates an earlier independent finding (Nordgaard et al., kappa 0.18). It cannot establish that structured interviews are invalid in general: n=55, one interviewer, one specialist unit, a non-random sample enriched for schizophrenia-spectrum presentations, and a protocol deliberately administered in a fully structured way without medical-record review, which the SCID User's Guide does not require. Rated moderate: a sound primary study with high ecological validity and limitations the authors state plainly, not a design that can carry a general claim.
