**Cannabis use and psychotic disorders in diverse settings in the Global South: findings from INTREPID II.**

DOI: 10.1017/S0033291723000399

## How much weight it bears: moderate

An individually matched case-control study nested in a population-based incidence programme, with cases ascertained by SCAN and exposure measured by the WHO ASSIST, which can establish that cannabis use was more common in cases than matched controls in each setting and that in Trinidad the association was graded by frequency and dependence. It cannot establish temporal order or causation, and in India and Nigeria cannabis use was too rare for any association to be estimated at all, so the paper supports no conclusion about those two settings. Moderate rather than strong because the design is cross-sectional in exposure and several Trinidad confidence intervals are wide or include 1.

*Graded on design-only.*
