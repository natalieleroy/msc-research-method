# Lewis-Fernández, R., Aggarwal, N. K., Lam, P. C., Galfalvy, H., Weiss, M. G., Kirmayer, L. J., Paralikar, V., Deshpande, S. N. et al. (2017)

**Feasibility, acceptability and clinical utility of the Cultural Formulation Interview: mixed-methods results from the DSM-5 international field trial.**

DOI: 10.1192/bjp.bp.116.193862

## How much weight it bears: moderate

A mixed-methods field trial across 14 sites in six countries, with 318 patients and 75 clinicians, which is the evidential basis on which the Cultural Formulation Interview was adopted into DSM-5; it can establish that a structured cultural assessment is feasible, acceptable and seen as clinically useful in routine practice, and it is the one source here with substantial non-Western and Global South participants (Kenya, India, Peru) rather than non-Western authors. It CANNOT establish that cultural formulation improves clinical outcomes: the outcomes are feasibility, acceptability and perceived utility, there is no control condition, and the paper reports that clinician feasibility ratings were significantly LOWER than patients', improving only after the first administration. Moderate rather than strong because the design is an implementation field trial without a comparator and because 'clinical utility' here is a rating, not an outcome.

*Graded on design-only.*
