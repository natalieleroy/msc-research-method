# Macneil, C. A., Hasty, M. K., Conus, P. & Berk, M. (2012)

**Is diagnosis enough to guide interventions in mental health? Using case formulation in clinical practice.**

DOI: 10.1186/1741-7015-10-111

## How much weight it bears: indicative

This is the citable peer-reviewed statement of the Five Ps framework, and it sets out all five headings — presenting problem, predisposing, precipitating, perpetuating and protective/positive factors — with worked clinical content under each. It can establish what the framework is, what each P is meant to contain, and that it is one approach among several rather than a validated instrument; the authors call it 'one of the more popular recent approaches', so it is a description of practice, not a derivation. It CANNOT establish that formulation improves outcomes: it is a debate and commentary piece with no data, no participants and no systematic search, and the authors themselves say research on the impact of formulation on outcome 'is still somewhat limited'. Indicative rather than moderate for that reason — the tier reflects the design, not the fit, which for `e2-plan` is excellent.
