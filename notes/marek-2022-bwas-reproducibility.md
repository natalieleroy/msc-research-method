**Reproducible brain-wide association studies require thousands of individuals**

DOI: 10.1038/s41586-022-04492-9

## How much weight it bears: strong

Peer-reviewed in Nature, open access, with named reviewers, open code and open data, and it uses the three largest neuroimaging datasets in existence (~50,000 people) rather than arguing from theory, so it can establish how large a sample a brain-wide association study needs before its effect sizes stop being inflated. It can establish that median univariate brain-behaviour effects are |r| = 0.01, that the largest effect replicating out of sample was |r| = 0.16, that at n = 25 the 99% confidence interval spans r +/- 0.52, and that at n < 500 replication rates were around 5%. It cannot establish anything about any specific disorder, it is cross-sectional and observational throughout, and the authors are explicit that it does NOT condemn small-sample neuroimaging in general — within-person, lesion and intervention designs have larger effects and are exempt. Several authors declare a financial interest in NOUS Imaging Inc., disclosed and managed. Tier is strong: adequately powered by construction, directly on the methodological claim, with the authors' own scope limits stated.
