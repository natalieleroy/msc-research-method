# Michalska da Rocha, B., Rhodes, S., Vasilopoulou, E. & Hutton, P. (2018)

**Loneliness in psychosis: a meta-analytical review.**

DOI: 10.1093/schbul/sbx036

## How much weight it bears: moderate

A random-effects meta-analysis of 13 studies and 15 647 participants with study and outcome quality assessed using adapted AHRQ and GRADE tools, finding a moderate association between loneliness and psychotic symptoms (r = .32, 95% CI 0.20-0.44) that did not depend on whether loneliness was measured by a single item or a full scale — a measurement-robustness check that matters, because the single-item objection is the obvious one. It can establish that loneliness and psychotic symptoms covary at a clinically meaningful magnitude, which is what lets social isolation be treated as a target rather than a backdrop. It CANNOT establish direction or cause: the authors say explicitly that 'further studies are needed to determine the causal status of this relationship', the included studies are correlational, and heterogeneity is extreme at I-squared = 97.56%, so the pooled r of .32 summarises a very dispersed set of estimates. The authors rate their own evidence 'moderate quality'.

*Graded on design-only.*
