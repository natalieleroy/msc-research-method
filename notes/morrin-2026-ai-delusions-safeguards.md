# Morrin, H., Nicholls, L., Levin, M., Yiend, J., Iyengar, U., DelGuidice, F., Bhattacharya, S., Tognin, S., MacCabe, J., Twumasi, R., Alderson-Day, B. & Pollak, T. A. (2026)

**Artificial intelligence-associated delusions and large language models: risks, mechanisms of delusion co-creation, and safeguarding strategies.**

DOI: 10.1016/S2215-0366(25)00396-7

## How much weight it bears: indicative

A Lancet Psychiatry Personal View, which is a high-visibility venue but an opinion format: no systematic search, no participants, no data of its own. It can establish three things that matter and that no study in the corpus establishes as crisply: that the current evidence indicates reinforcement of existing delusional beliefs rather than induction of de novo psychosis in the absence of vulnerability; that benefit and harm are both on the table, the AI agent sometimes functioning as 'a benign and predictable conversational anchor'; and that the proposed safeguards are UNTESTED, because the authors themselves say the protocols 'must be urgently co-designed with service users and clinicians and tested in clinical trials'. It CANNOT establish prevalence, mechanism or efficacy of anything, and its proposed framework of AI-informed care is a proposal. Indicative on design; its fit to `e2-mechanism`, `e2-evidence` and the education strand of `e2-plan` is excellent.
