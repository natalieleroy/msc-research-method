## How much weight it bears: indicative

A densely referenced 2026 narrative review in a peer-reviewed paediatrics journal, open access, which can establish what the current literature on adolescent generative AI use and mental health consists of, organise it by disorder, and supply the mechanisms the field is proposing - problematic and dependent use, sycophancy from reinforcement learning from human feedback, anthropomorphism, compensatory use, social displacement and cognitive offloading. Its most citable contribution is a limitation it states repeatedly in its own voice: the evidence is 'predominantly cross-sectional', consists 'predominantly of cross-sectional studies, case reports, and self-reported data, limiting the ability to establish temporality or causality', and the relationships 'may be bidirectional'. It cannot establish any association itself, because it conducts no analysis, and - the reason it is not rated moderate - it describes itself as a narrative review and **states no search strategy, no databases, no date range and no inclusion criteria**, so what it left out is unknowable.
