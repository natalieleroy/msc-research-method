# National Institute for Health and Care Excellence (2014)

**Psychosis and schizophrenia in adults: prevention and management. NICE clinical guideline CG178.**

DOI: not reported

## How much weight it bears: strong

A national clinical guideline developed through NICE's published process: commissioned systematic reviews, a multidisciplinary committee including service users and carers, consultation, and dated versioning with an audit trail of what changed and when. For the question 'what should a care plan for psychosis contain, and on whose authority', nothing outranks it in a UK context, and it is the document a UK marker will expect a holistic plan to be measured against. It establishes the expected SHAPE of care - oral antipsychotic plus family intervention and individual CBT at first episode, crisis resolution and home treatment as the first-line crisis service, supported employment, physical health monitoring, carer support, peer support and self-management - and it does so at the level of detail a 500-word plan can actually use. It establishes NOTHING about whether any of those interventions works: it is a consensus synthesis, so its recommendations are a committee's judgement on evidence, not evidence, and several load-bearing recommendations are marked [2009], meaning their last evidence review was in 2009. Essay 2 may cite it for what a plan should include and must not cite it for efficacy.
