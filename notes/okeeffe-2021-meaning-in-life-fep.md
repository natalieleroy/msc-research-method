# O'Keeffe, D., Keogh, B. & Higgins, A. (2021)

**Meaning in Life in Long-Term Recovery in First-Episode Psychosis: An Interpretative Phenomenological Analysis**

DOI: 10.3389/fpsyt.2021.676593

## How much weight it bears: moderate

A sound primary qualitative study: IPA conducted inside an epidemiologically complete FEP incidence cohort, with purposive maximum-variation sampling deliberately balanced across remission status, age at onset and gender, Yardley's four quality criteria applied and documented action by action, three analysis phases, personal and epistemological reflexivity reported, and the authors' own limitations stated at length. It answers what recovery and meaning are like from the service user's own standpoint two decades after a first episode - a question no outcome scale in this corpus asks - and it supplies an operational definition of first-episode psychosis and the remission criteria the cohort used. It cannot establish how common any of these experiences is, nor how meaning is experienced soon after onset, because every account is retrospective by about 21 years and the authors name recall bias themselves. Moderate rather than strong: 16 people, all Caucasian, mostly male, mostly unemployed, with possible recruitment bias towards those already interested in meaning in life, and the sample was not balanced across baseline diagnoses.

*Graded on full-appraisal.*
