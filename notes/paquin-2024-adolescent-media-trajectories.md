# Paquin, V., Ferrari, M., Rej, S., Boivin, M., Ouellet-Morin, I., Geoffroy, M. C. & Shah, J. L. (2024)

**Trajectories of Adolescent Media Use and Their Associations With Psychotic Experiences.**

DOI: 10.1001/jamapsychiatry.2024.0384

## How much weight it bears: strong

The only genuinely prospective source in this corpus on digital media use and psychotic experiences: a population birth cohort followed from 5 months to 23 years, with exposure reported at ages 12, 13, 15 and 17 and the outcome measured at 23, so the temporal order is fixed by design rather than by recall, and with adjustment for sociodemographic, genetic, family and childhood covariables measured between 5 months and 12 years. It can establish that adolescent media-use trajectories are at most MODESTLY associated with psychotic experiences in young adulthood, and - more usefully - that part of what looks like an effect is confounding by prior mental health: higher video gaming 'was preceded by higher levels of mental health and interpersonal problems at age 12 years', and adjusting for those 'mitigated the association'. It CANNOT support any claim about generative AI, smartphones or social media: the four exposures are television viewing, video gaming, computer use and reading, reported in a cohort born 1997-1998, so the exposure window is roughly 2010 to 2015 and predates the technology Essay 2 is about. Only ONE association survived adjustment - a curved trajectory of computer use, n=189 - and the authors' own conclusion attributes the findings 'likely' to shared risk factors. Strong on design; the fit to an AI claim is poor and must be stated.

*Graded on design-only.*
