# Penney, D., Sauvé, G., Mendelson, D., Thibaudeau, É., Moritz, S. & Lepage, M. (2022)

**Immediate and sustained outcomes and moderators associated with metacognitive training for psychosis: a systematic review and meta-analysis.**

DOI: 10.1001/jamapsychiatry.2022.0277

## How much weight it bears: strong

A PRISMA-reported meta-analysis of 43 studies, searching eleven databases from 2007 to June 2021, pooling 1816 participants and reporting Hedges g with 95% confidence intervals for proximal and distal outcomes, with effects maintained up to one year. It can establish that a structured intervention aimed at reasoning and cognitive biases reduces delusions (g = 0.69, 95% CI 0.45-0.93) and positive symptoms (g = 0.50, 95% CI 0.34-0.67) — a directly targeted, durable effect on the symptom class the vignette turns on. It CANNOT establish that it works by changing cognitive biases: the effect on cognitive biases themselves is the smallest significant one in the paper (g = 0.16, 95% CI 0.03-0.29), which undercuts the mechanism the intervention is named for; quality of life was non-significant (g = 0.20, 95% CI -0.07 to 0.47); and the co-developer of the intervention is an author, which is disclosed. Strong on design, with that mechanism finding and that conflict of interest both material.

*Graded on design-only.*
