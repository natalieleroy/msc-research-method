# Penttilä, M., Jääskeläinen, E., Hirvonen, N., Isohanni, M. & Miettunen, J. (2014)

**Duration of untreated psychosis as predictor of long-term outcome in schizophrenia: systematic review and meta-analysis**

DOI: 10.1192/bjp.bp.113.127753

## How much weight it bears: moderate

A PRISMA-structured systematic review and random-effects meta-analysis across seven databases, including non-English publications professionally translated, with stricter diagnostic inclusion than previous meta-analyses, heterogeneity quantified per outcome category, meta-regression on six study-level covariates, and a sensitivity analysis restricted to unadjusted correlations. It can establish that duration of untreated psychosis is associated with worse long-term symptomatic, remission, social-functioning and global outcome, and - just as usefully - that it is **not** significantly associated with employment, quality of life or hospital treatment. It cannot establish that treatment delay causes the worse outcome: every included study is observational, the majority of pooled correlations are unadjusted for confounders, and the authors say the correlation is 'small but mostly consistent'. Moderate rather than strong: only 7 to 19 samples per outcome category, moderate to high heterogeneity in six of nine categories, withdrawal of 4-71% with five samples losing over half their participants, no included study used any statistical method to account for attrition, and the authors had to convert the original effect-size measures into correlations.

*Graded on full-appraisal.*
