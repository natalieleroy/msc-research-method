# Reiss, F. (2013)

**Socioeconomic inequalities and mental health problems in children and adolescents: A systematic review.**

DOI: 10.1016/j.socscimed.2013.04.026

## How much weight it bears: moderate

A systematic review that can establish a consistent inverse gradient between socioeconomic status and mental health problems in children and adolescents — 52 of 55 included studies found it, disadvantaged young people being two to three times more likely to develop mental health problems — and can establish that persistent low SES and declining SES are both worse than transient disadvantage, which is a dose and duration finding the corpus otherwise lacks. It CANNOT establish anything about psychosis: the outcome throughout is 'mental health problems', measured by general psychopathology screens, and the review does not disaggregate psychotic experiences. The author lists publication bias and the diversity of SES and outcome measures as limitations, and states that excluding longitudinal studies of over-18s 'restricted the study's ability to observe causal associations over the life course'. Tier is moderate on quality; its FIT for a psychosis essay is POOR.
