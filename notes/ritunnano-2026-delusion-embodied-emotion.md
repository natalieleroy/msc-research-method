# Ritunnano, R., Littlemore, J., Nelson, B., Humpston, C. S. & Broome, M. R. (2026)

**Delusion as embodied emotion: a qualitatively driven, multimethod study of first-episode psychosis in the UK**

DOI: 10.1016/S2215-0366(25)00341-4

## How much weight it bears: moderate

A sound primary qualitative study, peer reviewed in The Lancet Psychiatry, open access under CC BY, reported against the APA Journal Article Reporting Standards for Qualitative Research, with the design and protocol developed with a group of people with lived experience, a prior systematic search of the qualitative literature reported in Research in Context, and three analytical standpoints held apart before integration. It answers a question no quantitative design in this corpus asks: what the emergence of a delusion is like from inside, and where its thematic content comes from in the person's own life narrative. It cannot establish prevalence, cause or sequence in any population - the authors state plainly that the study 'does not enable universal generalisations beyond the context and conditions of this study' - and it cannot speak for people who cannot sustain long verbal interviews. Moderate rather than strong because it is a ten-person idiographic study with limitations the authors name themselves: a high proportion of White participants, mostly female, selected for a degree of articulacy.

*Graded on full-appraisal.*
