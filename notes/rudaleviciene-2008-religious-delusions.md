# Rudalevičienė, P., Stompe, T., Narbekovas, A., Raškauskienė, N. & Bunevičius, R. (2008)

**Are religious delusions related to religiosity in schizophrenia?**

DOI: 10.3390/medicina44070068

## How much weight it bears: moderate

The only study found that regresses delusional CONTENT on named predictors and reports odds ratios with confidence intervals, which is the form of evidence a pathoplasticity claim needs and which the rest of the content literature does not supply. It CAN establish, in 295 Lithuanian inpatients assessed with a named content instrument, that religious delusional content was independently predicted by being divorced or separated rather than married (OR 2.0, 95% CI 1.1-3.5, P=0.019) and by having some postsecondary education (OR 2.3, 95% CI 1.4-3.9, P=0.002), while personal religiosity was NOT an independent predictor (adjusted OR 1.7, 95% CI 0.8-3.8) — so the obvious candidate for what shapes religious content does not survive adjustment, and social position does. It also documents that content is gender-patterned: women most often believed they were saints, men that they were God. It CANNOT establish direction, which the authors state plainly, nor much explanatory power: the model's Nagelkerke R-squared is 0.12, so 88% of the variation in whether someone had religious delusions is unaccounted for. Diagnoses were clinical rather than research-interview confirmed, by the authors' own admission. Moderate: full text read, a validated structured instrument, effect sizes with intervals and a genuinely counter-intuitive adjusted result, limited by a cross-sectional single-centre design, unverified diagnoses, a printed confidence interval that cannot be right (see Reader's note) and low model fit.
