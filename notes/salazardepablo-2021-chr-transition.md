# Salazar de Pablo, G., Radua, J., Pereira, J., Bonoldi, I., Arienti, V., Besana, F., Soardo, L., Cabras, A., Fortea, L., Catalan, A., Vaquerizo-Serrano, J., Coronelli, F., Kaur, S., Da Silva, J., Shin, J. I., Solmi, M., Brondino, N., Politi, P., McGuire, P. & Fusar-Poli, P. (2021)

**Probability of Transition to Psychosis in Individuals at Clinical High Risk: An Updated Meta-analysis.**

DOI: 10.1001/jamapsychiatry.2021.0830

## How much weight it bears: strong

A PRISMA- and MOOSE-compliant meta-analysis of 130 longitudinal studies and 9222 individuals, with independent data extraction, digitisation of published Kaplan-Meier curves to recover survival data, heterogeneity analysis, study-quality assessment and meta-regression. It can establish, with unusual precision for this literature, what proportion of people meeting clinical high-risk criteria go on to develop psychosis and over what period. It cannot establish what predicts transition in an individual, and it inherits the selection bias of the help-seeking samples that clinical high-risk services recruit, so the rates do not transfer to the general population.

*Graded on design-only.*
