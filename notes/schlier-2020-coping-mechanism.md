# Schlier, B., Ludwig, L., Wiesjahn, M., Jung, E. & Lincoln, T. M. (2020)

**Fostering coping as a mechanism of symptom change in cognitive behavioural therapy for psychosis**

DOI: 10.1016/j.schres.2019.07.047

## How much weight it bears: indicative

The only source found so far that tests the direction of the coping-symptom relationship rather than assuming it: weekly self-assessments across up to 45 sessions, analysed by multilevel models, with a reverse model fitted as well, which is the right design for a mechanism question and is why it outranks Strous (2005) and MacAulay & Cohen (2013) for this claim despite being smaller. It establishes temporal precedence in an uncontrolled, within-person, treatment-improvement direction: improvement in coping preceded improvement in suspiciousness, negative symptoms and depression. It cannot establish causation or generalise to exacerbation, because it is a secondary analysis of a single uncontrolled therapy sample of 57 people, all measures are self-report, and the mediator and outcome share method and informant. Indicative on sample size, secondary-analysis status and abstract-only access; the design is the reason it is usable at all.

*Graded on design-only.*
