# Stefana, A., Oliver, D., Estrade, A., Azis, M., Damiani, S., Fusar-Poli, L., Brondino, N., Provenzani, U., Bonoldi, I., Uhlhaas, P. J. & Fusar-Poli, P. (2026)

**The Mini-CAARMS: Development and Validation of a Short Version of the Comprehensive Assessment of AT Risk Mental States to Facilitate Preventive Psychiatry.**

DOI: 10.1093/schbul/sbaf146

## How much weight it bears: moderate

A validation study on 490 community participants across three independent UK and Italian sites, abbreviating the 60-item CAARMS/2006 positive-symptom domain to 23 items using LASSO within nested cross-validation, with Cohen's kappa, Harrell's C, sensitivity, specificity, predictive values and a Monte Carlo simulation of two-year prognostic accuracy. It is the best source now in the corpus for what a clinician-administered psychosis-risk interview actually CONSISTS of: 60 questions in four aggregate scales, each item rated for severity, frequency, pattern, distress and onset/offset, requiring extensive clinician training and often two hours to administer. Its agreement findings are internal rather than external: the short form is derived from and tested against the same 490 interviews, so kappa 0.90 measures self-consistency with the parent instrument, not accuracy against clinical outcome. The reported specificity of 100% and PPV of 100% are artefacts of the scoring rule, which the authors state plainly in the body and the abstract does not. Prognostic accuracy is simulated, not observed: the authors state they have no follow-up data for this cohort. Rated moderate on that combination of a real multi-site sample and a derivation design that cannot establish prediction.
