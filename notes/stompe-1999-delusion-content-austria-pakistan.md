# Stompe, T., Friedman, A., Ortwein, G., Strobl, R., Chaudhry, H. R., Najam, N. & Chaudhry, M. R. (1999)

**Comparison of delusions among schizophrenics in Austria and in Pakistan**

DOI: 10.1159/000029094

## How much weight it bears: moderate

The answer to the question of whether anyone has coded delusional content against a predictor rather than against the calendar: 234 patients in two countries, delusional contents classified using the scheme of Huber and Gross, with culture as the comparison. It CAN establish that content differs systematically by culture — significantly higher frequencies of delusions of grandeur, guilt and religious delusions in the Austrian sample — and, crucially for a pathoplasticity argument, that the differences extend to the KIND of persecution and the TYPE OF PERSECUTOR, which were registered as separate variables. That is the only study found anywhere that treats the identity of the persecutor as a codeable, comparable variable, which is the exact slot a chatbot would occupy. It CANNOT establish that any particular agent, technology or stressor produces a particular content: culture here is a two-level grouping variable standing in for everything that differs between Vienna and Lahore, with no mechanism tested and no adjustment reported in the abstract. Nor can it speak to technology at all, in 1999. Moderate: a purpose-designed two-country comparison on a validated content classification with an adequate clinical sample, limited by a crude exposure variable, no reported confounding control, a 1999 diagnostic framework (DSM-III-R), and a record read from the abstract only because the paper is paywalled.

*Graded on design-only.*
