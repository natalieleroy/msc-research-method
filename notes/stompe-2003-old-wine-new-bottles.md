# Stompe, T., Ortwein-Swoboda, G., Ritter, K. & Schanda, H. (2003)

**Old wine in new bottles? Stability and plasticity of the contents of schizophrenic delusions**

DOI: 10.1159/000069658

## How much weight it bears: indicative

A narrative review that takes on, by name and twenty-three years early, the exact question Essay 2 inherits from Bokhari et al. (2026): whether contemporary technologies supply new delusional content or only new vocabulary. Its answer is the second, and it is the most directly disconfirming source in the corpus for the strong reading of pathoplasticity. It CAN establish that the question has a prior literature with a reasoned answer, and it supplies the conceptual apparatus for that answer — Klosterkotter's three-stage model of delusion formation, in which the 'new' technological material appears only at the third stage, concretisation, over a small set of basic themes (persecution, grandiosity, guilt, religion, hypochondria, jealousy, love) of 'extraordinary anthropological importance'. It also states that persecution and grandiosity showed NO variability over time or between cultures. It CANNOT establish any of this empirically on its own account: it is a narrative synthesis with no stated search strategy, no inclusion criteria, no quality appraisal and no new data, and it reasons from case reports plus the authors' own transcultural work, so its conclusion is an argument rather than a result. Indicative: conceptually load-bearing, methodologically a review article from one group reconciling a literature that includes its own prior studies.
