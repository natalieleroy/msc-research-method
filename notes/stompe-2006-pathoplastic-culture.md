# Stompe, T., Karakula, H., Rudalevičiene, P., Okribelashvili, N., Chaudhry, H. R., Idemudia, E. E. & Gscheider, S. (2006)

**The pathoplastic effect of culture on psychotic symptoms in schizophrenia**

DOI: not reported

## How much weight it bears: indicative

The only study found that puts a NUMBER on pathoplasticity — the share of variance in delusional content, hallucination modality and first-rank symptoms attributable to culture of origin, across 1080 patients in seven countries by canonical discriminant analysis — and the design would support a moderate rating. Its reporting does not. The paper states its one headline figure in THREE mutually inconsistent forms: the abstract says 'rates between 15% and 40%', the results say 'the average correct assignment of psychotic phenomena to countries of origin is between 16 and 40%', and the discussion says 'Between 15% and 30% of the frequencies of contents of delusions, modalities of hallucinations and first rank symptoms may be caused by differences in the cultures of origin'. No single defensible value can be quoted from it. What it CAN carry, robustly, is the complement and the floor: between 70% and 85% of the variance in psychotic symptom prevalence does NOT depend on culture of origin, and chance classification across seven countries is 14.3%, which the authors call a 'gray zone' their estimate sits only a little above. It also records that the term pathoplasticity itself 'has a more or less metaphoric character'. It CANNOT support any causal or mechanistic claim, and it is the same 1080 patients as its companion paper, so the two do not corroborate each other. Indicative: a strong design whose only quantitative claim is reported three ways, in a journal with no DOI.
