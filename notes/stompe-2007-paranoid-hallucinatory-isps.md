# Stompe, T., Bauer, S., Karakula, H., Rudaleviciene, P., Okribelashvili, N., Chaudhry, H. R., Idemudia, E. E. & Gschaider, S. (2007)

**Paranoid-hallucinatory syndromes in schizophrenia: results of the International Study on Psychotic Symptoms**

DOI: not reported

## How much weight it bears: moderate

This is the source that answers the project's decisive question, and it answers it by enumerating an instrument rather than by arguing. It CAN establish, because it prints the taxonomy in full, that the FPS — the instrument behind the largest cross-cultural study of delusional content located anywhere, 1080 consecutively admitted inpatients in seven countries — codes delusional content as exactly TEN binary themes: persecution, poisoning, grandeur, religion, hypochondria, guilt, apocalypse, erotomania, jealousy, descent. Not one is technological, and there is no persecutor-identity category at any level: identity is captured only in a free 'descriptive part' alongside the binary codes. It can also establish that six of seven principal-component syndromes derived from those themes were unequally distributed across the seven sites, delusional jealousy excepted. It CANNOT bear weight on the factor structure itself: the PCA is exploratory with seven factors explaining 11.4% down to 6.5% of variance each, no confirmatory replication, and the paper's own narrative description of factors 4 and 5 contradicts the names it then assigns them. Nor can it speak to technology, in 2007. Moderate: a large, consecutive, multi-site sample with SCID-derived consensus diagnoses and a fully documented instrument, read in full, limited by exploratory factor analysis, several printed inconsistencies, and publication in a small society journal whose peer-review process is evidenced only by printed received and accepted dates.

*Graded on full-appraisal.*
