# Strous, R. D., Ratner, Y., Gibel, A., Ponizovsky, A. & Ritsner, M. (2005)

**Longitudinal assessment of coping abilities at exacerbation and stabilization in schizophrenia**

DOI: 10.1016/j.comppsych.2004.07.035

## How much weight it bears: moderate

A genuinely longitudinal clinical cohort - 237 patients assessed in an acute exacerbation and 148 of them again at stabilization across 16 months - with a validated coping instrument and a wide battery of clinical and psychosocial measures, which is a stronger design than anything else currently available to this project on coping in psychosis. What it establishes is that emotion-oriented coping is used more during exacerbation than during stabilization, and that emotional distress, self-efficacy and social support predict coping style at both phases. What it cannot establish is the direction the argument needs: coping is the DEPENDENT variable in every regression reported, symptom severity is a predictor of it, and symptom severity accounts for only 3.5% of the variance in emotion-oriented coping and 5.5-9% in task-oriented coping. Moderate on design; attrition from 237 to 148 is 37.6% and unexplained in the abstract, and the record is abstract-only.

*Graded on design-only.*
