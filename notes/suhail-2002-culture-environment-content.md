# Suhail, K. & Cochrane, R. (2002)

**Effect of culture and environment on the phenomenology of delusions and hallucinations**

DOI: 10.1177/002076402128783181

## How much weight it bears: moderate

The best-designed study found on whether delusional content tracks a predictor, because its three-group design does what a simple two-country comparison cannot: by holding ethnicity constant across two environments (Pakistanis in Birmingham versus Pakistanis in Lahore) and environment constant across two ethnicities (Pakistanis versus British White in Birmingham), it separates culture of origin from immediate environment. It CAN establish that content differed more between the two Pakistani groups than between the two Birmingham groups, which the authors read as a stronger influence of the immediate environment than of inherited culture — the most directly pathoplasticity-relevant empirical result located anywhere, and from a team independent of the Vienna group that produced everything else on this question. It also names a content-coding instrument: all references to delusions and hallucinations were coded with the classification scheme of the Present State Examination (Wing et al., 1974). It CANNOT establish any mechanism, and its ascertainment is its weakest point: content was taken from medical records and key-worker reports rather than from direct interview, so what is compared is partly what clinicians in two health systems chose to write down. Moderate: a genuinely informative design on a named coding scheme with an adequate sample, limited by retrospective record-based ascertainment, cross-sectional timing, group sizes of 50-98, and a record read from the abstract only because the paper is paywalled.

*Graded on design-only.*
