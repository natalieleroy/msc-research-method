# Tapiavala, V., Chilakapati, S. S., Moore, D., Morales, S., Kumaran, A., Heller, R., Werlin, J. & Fortuna, K. L. (2026)

**Perspectives of People with Serious Mental Illness on Artificial Intelligence-Based Companions: \"If no one told you I love you today, you have someone that cares for you.\**

DOI: 10.1007/s10597-026-01732-4

## How much weight it bears: indicative

Peer reviewed in Community Mental Health Journal, and as far as any search has found it is the only study anywhere that asks people with psychotic disorders directly what they think about AI companions - which is why it is worth having on the record at all. It can indicate what concerns and conditions of trust this population articulates: strong concerns about data privacy and misuse, recognition of potential support for reminders, mental health and daily functioning, greater trust where tools were personalised, human-centred and co-developed, and residual worry about loss of critical thinking and overreliance. It cannot establish how common any of those views are, nor separate the views of people with schizophrenia spectrum disorder from those with bipolar disorder or major depression, because the abstract reports no participant count and no diagnostic breakdown. Indicative rather than moderate for two reasons stated in the abstract itself: it is a **secondary analysis** of three focus groups collected for another purpose, and the full text could not be reached to appraise the sampling, coding or reflexivity.

*Graded on design-only.*
