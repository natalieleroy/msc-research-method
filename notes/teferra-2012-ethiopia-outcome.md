# Teferra, S., Shibre, T., Fekadu, A., Medhin, G., Wakwoya, A., Alem, A. & Jacobsson, L. (2012)

**Five-year clinical course and outcome of schizophrenia in Ethiopia.**

DOI: 10.1016/j.schres.2011.10.021

## How much weight it bears: moderate

A population-based prospective cohort with five-year follow-up, built by screening 68 378 adults rather than by sampling clinic attenders, in a setting where 89.6% of cases were treatment naive at entry — a design that is now almost impossible to repeat and that directly tests the long-standing claim of better schizophrenia outcomes in low-income countries. It can establish that the WHO 'better outcome in developing countries' finding does not hold here: 45% were continuously symptomatic, 30.3% had a continuous psychotic episode and only about 20% had continuous remission, and it can establish that being on antipsychotic treatment for at least half the follow-up predicted longer remission (OR 2.28, 95% CI 1.12-4.62). It CANNOT establish that treatment caused the better outcome: the treatment exposure is observational, so reverse causation and confounding by severity are open, and the cohort was largely chronic at entry so it is not a first-episode study. Moderate: strong design for its question, non-first-episode population, observational treatment comparison.

*Graded on design-only.*
