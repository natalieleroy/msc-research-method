# Wunderink, L., Nieboer, R. M., Wiersma, D., Sytema, S. & Nienhuis, F. J. (2013)

**Recovery in remitted first-episode psychosis at 7 years of follow-up of an early dose reduction/discontinuation or maintenance treatment strategy: long-term follow-up of a 2-year randomized clinical trial.**

DOI: 10.1001/jamapsychiatry.2013.19

## How much weight it bears: moderate

A seven-year follow-up of a randomised trial in remitted first-episode psychosis with 80.5% of the original randomised sample located, reporting that the dose-reduction/discontinuation arm had twice the recovery rate of the maintenance arm (40.4% versus 17.6%, OR 3.49, P = .01), driven by functional rather than symptomatic remission. It is the only source found that disconfirms the straightforward 'stay on medication' reading of the relapse evidence, and it does so in exactly the vignette's population — a first episode, in remission. It CANNOT bear a recommendation: the randomisation governed only the first 18 months and treatment after the 2-year trial was 'at the discretion of the clinician', so the seven-year comparison is observational in all but name; it was an open (unblinded) trial; 111 of 257 eligible patients refused, which is a severe selection problem; n is 103 and the primary outcome was not the original trial's; and the authors say 'additional studies are necessary before these results are incorporated into general practice'. Moderate, and the reason is the 18-month randomisation window, not the finding.

*Graded on design-only.*
