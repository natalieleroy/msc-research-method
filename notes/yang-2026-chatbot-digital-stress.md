# Yang, Q., Wu, H., Du, X., Sun, Q. & Ren, Z. (2026)

**Emotional support or problem-solving? A randomized controlled trial of function-specific AI chatbots for digital stress in college students.**

DOI: 10.1111/aphw.70208

## How much weight it bears: indicative

A genuine three-arm randomised controlled trial with a waitlist control in the vignette's population — university students, selected for elevated digital stress — which is a design the AI-and-mental-health corpus otherwise does not contain at all, and with Chinese participants, which the corpus badly needs. It can establish that a 14-day self-guided generative-AI chatbot can reduce specific dimensions of digital stress, and that function matters: the emotional-support bot reduced availability stress, approval anxiety, social comparison stress and fear of missing out, while the problem-solving bot was better at reducing online vigilance. It CANNOT bear weight on psychosis: there is no psychosis or psychosis-risk outcome anywhere in it, the sample is 99 across three arms so roughly 33 per cell, the follow-up is 14 days, and 'both interventions significantly alleviated anxiety symptoms, whereas effects on depressive symptoms were not significant' — a split result the essay should report rather than round up. Indicative: strong design, very small, very short, and wrong outcome for the claim it would be asked to support.

*Graded on design-only.*
