# Zhang, Y., Zhao, D., Hancock, J. T., Kraut, R. & Yang, D. (2026)

**Interaction with AI companions and psychological well-being**

DOI: 10.1038/s41562-026-02516-2

## How much weight it bears: moderate

The only source found that separates companionship use of a chatbot from instrumental use in real users and attaches an outcome to the distinction, and it does so with behavioural evidence — 464687 messages from donated chat logs — rather than self-report alone, which is what the rest of this corpus runs on. It CAN establish that among 1131 Character.AI users the association between AI companionship and well-being is conditional: reporting companionship as the primary use was associated with lower well-being (beta -0.48, 95% CI -0.70 to -0.25), more so when interactions were intensive (-0.31, CI -0.56 to -0.06) and highly disclosive (-0.38, CI -0.63 to -0.14), and smaller offline social networks were associated with reporting companionship as the primary use (beta -0.03, CI -0.05 to -0.01). It CANNOT establish direction or drift: the design is cross-sectional, so a small social network preceding companionship use and companionship use shrinking a social network fit the data equally, and nothing in it tracks a person moving from instrumental to companion use over time. Nor can it bear weight on psychosis: there is no psychosis or psychosis-risk measure. Moderate: a large, well-reported study in a strong venue with behavioural triangulation and confidence intervals, limited by a self-selected sample of users of one product, a US-only frame, a cross-sectional design, and a record read from the abstract only because the paper is paywalled.

*Graded on design-only.*
