# Zhu, Y., Krause, M., Huhn, M., Rothe, P., Schneider-Thoma, J., Chaimani, A., Li, C., Davis, J. M. & Leucht, S. (2017)

**Antipsychotic drugs for the acute treatment of patients with a first episode of schizophrenia: a systematic review with pairwise and network meta-analyses.**

DOI: 10.1016/S2215-0366(17)30270-5

## How much weight it bears: moderate

The dedicated first-episode evidence synthesis for acute antipsychotic treatment: 19 RCTs, 12 drugs, 2669 participants, with SMDs and ORs and 95% CIs, searching seven databases plus ClinicalTrials.gov without language restriction. It can establish that haloperidol is a suboptimal first choice in first-episode schizophrenia, that differences between second-generation drugs are small, and — the finding that matters most for a treatment plan — that 'the choice of treatment should be guided primarily by side-effects'. It CANNOT support a claim that any particular drug is best: the authors state the evidence was 'generally of low quality' and 'the numbers of patients for each drug were small', the significant effects against haloperidol are small (SMD -0.14 to -0.37) with intervals reaching -0.01, and tolerability findings are graded very low to low quality. Moderate rather than strong on the authors' own quality assessment of the underlying trials.

*Graded on design-only.*
